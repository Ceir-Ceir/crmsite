<H2> 1. No Work Before Confirming Safety of the Excavation </H2>

The first and most important rule in excavation is that no workflow should commence until the excavation site is safe. To ensure this, the commercial client should provide vital information to the contractor prior to any work. This may include potential hazards, site condition, and any relevant data that contributes to the safety and efficiency of the excavation work.

At CRM, we prioritize safety by ensuring we have all necessary information before any operations. Moreover, we integrate advanced technology in our operation process to ascertain efficient and diligent execution, even in challenging commercial and industrial excavation projects.

<H2> 2. Implementing Effective Engineering Controls </H2>

SafetyCulture recommends benching, battering, or shoring the sides of the excavation to reduce the risk of ground collapse, which is a common safety hazard in excavation work. In addition to this, administrative controls should be in place to minimize any remaining risks. This could involve clear signage near the excavation site warning of potential hazards.

At CRM, our experienced excavation crews follow a strict adherence to the best engineering controls to ensure the stability of the ground during our operations, be it demolition work, dumpster rental, or concrete washouts.

<H2> 3. Stay Up to Date with OSHA Trenching and Excavation Standards </H2>

Knowledge is critical in managing excavation safety hazards. As a San Diego-based company, CRM stays abreast of the Occupational Safety and Health Administration (OSHA) trenching and excavation standards.
